Guides

Short, practical guides for the jobs people most often need to do with their documents. Everything happens on your own device.

  • Remove a CPR number from a PDFA CPR number on a document you share can be misused. Here is how to remove it properly, so it is really gone from the file and not just hidden.
  • Share a payslip safelyLandlords, banks and new employers often ask to see a payslip. They need your income, not your CPR number or bank account. Here is how to send only what they need.
  • Sign a rental contract without printingYou have received a lease as a PDF and need to sign it. You do not need a printer and a scanner: you can sign it on your computer or phone in a minute.
  • Collect receipts in one PDFInsurance claims, expense reports and tax deductions often need the receipts as one file. Here is how to go from a pile of paper to one tidy PDF, using just your phone.
  • Turn iPhone photos (HEIC) into JPGiPhones save photos as HEIC to save space. That is fine on the phone, but many Windows PCs, websites and forms will not take them. Here is how to turn them into ordinary JPG photos.
  • Make a PDF small enough to e-mailYour PDF is too big to attach. Most mail services stop at 20 to 25 MB, and some companies set an even lower limit. Here is how to make it small enough.
  • Remove the location from photos before you share themMost phone photos quietly record where they were taken. A photo of the sofa you are selling can tell a stranger where you live. Here is how to remove that before you share.
  • Turn a PDF into Word and keep the layoutYou need to change a document you only have as a PDF. Here is how to turn it into a Word file you can edit, with the fonts, bold text, alignment and pictures kept.
  • PDF too big to upload? Make it fit the limitGovernment portals, job sites and insurance claims often refuse files over a few megabytes, and the error message rarely helps. Here is how to get under the limit.
  • Combine scanned pages into one PDFScanners and phone apps often give you one file per page, or all the fronts and then all the backs. Here is how to end up with one tidy PDF, in the right order.
